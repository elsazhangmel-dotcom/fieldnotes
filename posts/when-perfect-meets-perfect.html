When perfect meets perfect

July 22, 2026 · 2 minute read

Are perfect answers generated by AI any less real? guess it’s just like lab created diamonds vs natural ones, sure timeline is compressed but composition identical. Timeline is a building variable not an outcome, by which measure it is no less real than human created answers.

Yet market rewards natural diamonds a much higher price still. It is no secret even for natural diamonds market the price was artificially inflated by supply control. The seemingly market irrationality hides real value derived from the creative process even if outcome is the same.

Many of us have experienced this cheating feeling when first started using AI for work. As humans we place value on our peers’ endeavours. Consider hand made goods, often with worse quality (just try a scrunchie that was made by hand vs by machine) commands a higher price in market. This line of thinking is uncontentional in software business, where value of the tool needs to be backed by the amount of things it can do, human endeavour does not command a price, usually. Managed service’s value lies in software adding human effort resulting in solution. Different from the scrunchie analogy, where human endeavour is priced for its trouble to make the thing, not adding on top of it.

Let us assume this is true and transferrable to software making. What angle might we be able to add the flavour of human endeavour to the price? Could ‘human reviewed’ label command a price premium just like “organic”?
