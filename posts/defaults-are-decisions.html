Defaults are decisions

July 18, 2026 · 2 minute read

Most users never change the defaults. That means the default is not a neutral starting point — it is the product’s real opinion, wearing a quieter outfit.

Teams debate features for weeks, then leave the first-run experience to habit. The checkbox that arrives already checked, the sort order no one questions, the empty state that pushes one action over another: these shape behavior more than the settings page ever will.

A useful question before shipping: if ninety percent of people keep this as-is, are we comfortable with the world that creates?

Defaults are product strategy with fewer words.

Treat them like a feature. Write them down. Defend them. Change them when the evidence says the silent majority deserves better.
