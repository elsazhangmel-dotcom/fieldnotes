A walk without a phone

July 22, 2026 · 2 minute read

I left the phone on the kitchen table and walked for an hour. The first ten minutes felt oddly incomplete, as if I had forgotten a necessary tool.

Then the incompleteness became the point. Without a camera, the trees stayed trees. Without a map, the route became a series of choices instead of instructions.

I noticed the temperature change under the canopy. A dog barked two streets over. Someone was practicing piano with the window open. None of it was content. All of it was life.

What came back

Ideas arrive differently when you are not harvesting them. They show up sideways, while you are watching your shoes or waiting at a light. You remember them later, or you don’t — and both outcomes are fine.

Attention is not a resource to optimize. It is a place to live.

I will not make a ritual out of phoneless walks. But I want more hours where nothing is being captured, and something is still being kept.
